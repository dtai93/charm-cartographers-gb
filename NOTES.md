Biscuit Isle is one joke carried through the whole chart. The land is a shortbread with a bite taken out of the north-east, the river is tea, the treasure is the last biscuit, and the kraken is a teapot. The teaspoon compass and the league of three biscuits belong to the same story.

Session 4 changed the sheet from a flat diagram into a painted chart:

- The page is warm cream parchment with brown stains, a burnt edge and short fibre marks. The sea is a blotchy teal glaze so the paper still shows, deeper offshore, with four ink ripples along the coast and a few wave ticks.
- The island is biscuit gold with a baked rim, soft green lowland patches, three rows of fork-prick holes, hatching on the south-east slope and three clusters of tree doodles.
- Dunking Lagoon is a tea-brown oval with a pale rim and two ripple rings. Jammy Marsh is a jam wash with reeds and two jam jars. Mount Kettle is a shaded cone with a spout and three steam puffs. The Steeping River runs from the kettle’s foot, narrow to wide, into the lagoon.
- Saucer Bay is a real scoop in the south coast. The ship sits in the bay, the note sits beside it, and the walk starts at a rowing boat on the beach.
- The dashed walk goes beach, tea break (with a cup), Sugar Lump Bridge, the lagoon’s west side, a napkin loop, then the X in a hollow. “The Last Biscuit” sits with that X. The other notes sit by the cliffs, the kettle’s steam and the water of Bite Bay.
- The Great Teapot Kraken is in the south-west sea with “Here Be Dunkers” under its name. The compass is an eight-point rose whose north point is a teaspoon. The scale is three biscuit rounds. A corked bottle and two gulls sit in the open sea.

Session 5, final polish:

- The sea is an even translucent teal wash, pale along the shore and deeper offshore, with fine grain and a few faint streaks. Coast ripples stay. The wedge-shaped fans in Bite Bay are gone; the bay has three smooth ink arcs.
- The Great Teapot Kraken sits fully in the south-west sea, about 220px wide on the chart: lid, knob, spout, three steam puffs, handle, eyes, smile, and five curling tentacles. “The Great Teapot Kraken” and “Here Be Dunkers” sit just beneath it, inside the frame.
- Mount Kettle is a lumpy inked cone with a dark crater, hatched shadow, a flank spout, and steam drifting up toward “Boils at four o’clock sharp”. The Steeping River still leaves its foot.
- Labels were pulled off the coastline, the X, the legend bottle, and each other. “The Custard Creams” sits under the two biscuit rocks. The legend reads “the Steeping River”.
